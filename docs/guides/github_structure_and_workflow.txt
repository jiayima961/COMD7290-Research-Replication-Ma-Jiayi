3. HOW THE FILES CONNECT

sources/: establishes what the article, assignment, and author materials say.
docs/: explains the theory, measurement, design, models, and interpretation rules.
data/: supplies permitted original inputs and programmatically derived data.
analysis/: records how inputs are transformed into estimates and comparisons.
tests/: checks whether inputs and analysis obey reviewed substantive rules.
outputs/: contains actual results, diagnostics, comparisons, and review evidence.
records/: identifies sources, versions, tasks, decisions, approvals, and execution.
README.md: connects each hypothesis to its specification, code, output, and conclusion.
report/: explains the most consequential reasoning and findings concisely.

The central chain is:
theory/hypothesis -> measurement/model -> data/code -> executed evidence ->
independent verification -> scientific conclusion.
hypothesis_matrix.csv traces that chain for every required analytical target.

4. PREPARATION RULES

1) Required local materials are not automatically shareable. Check permissions for
   data, PDFs, author code, and outputs. When redistribution is not allowed or not
   yet verified, keep originals local and document lawful access and limitations.
2) Create/adapt at least three project skills and actually invoke and test them.
   Each SKILL.md needs name/description metadata, inputs, procedure, outputs,
   validation, and stopping rules. At least one must address the selected article's
   advanced measurement or model. Files alone do not prove discovery or invocation.
3) Choose software from the actual analytical requirements. R, Python, Stata,
   Mplus, or MATLAB may be appropriate; document equivalence before switching engines.
4) Implement one documented entry command rebuilding all permitted results and
   comparisons. Test a clean restart without hidden notebook/chat state.
5) Keep published targets separate from actual refitted estimates. Label small
   diagnostic runs separately from complete final runs.

5. WORKFLOW

STEP 1 - Select the article and establish scope
Compare interest, material access, permissions, software, and runtime. Inventory
the whole article and all relevant supplements rather than selecting easy tests.
Current decision: Option 3 selected.
Outputs: selection decision, full input/analysis inventory, unresolved-input list.

STEP 2 - Initialize the project and GitHub access
Create a personal private repository and essential documentation/source records.
The assignment asks for an early collaborator invitation to fffchaobu. The user's
current explicit instruction postpones that invitation; record it accurately.
Check software and permissions and conduct a small authorized execution test.
Outputs: navigable repository and dated Checkpoint A feasibility evidence.

STEP 3 - Reconstruct the scientific argument
Read the article, supplements, codebooks, and at least two cited foundational
theoretical or methodological sources. First draft your own theory map and
hypothesis-to-analysis explanation, then ask Codex to critique and revise against
primary-source section/table/code locators. Keep author decisions, reconstruction,
and criticism distinct. Keep exploratory questions exploratory. Compare any
preregistration with the article; a post-publication plan is not an original preregistration.
Outputs: theory document, original preliminary map, hypothesis implications.

STEP 4 - Specify measurement, samples, and models for student review
Define one row, sampling/coding units, dependence, coding, exclusions, missingness,
estimands, equations, settings, assumptions, intervals, and interpretation rules.
Complete the matrix, dictionary, and diagram before substantive estimation.
Outputs: reviewed, versioned specification and Checkpoint B approval record.

STEP 5 - Establish skills, functions, and handoffs
Separate theory/specification review, data/measurement analysis, model implementation,
and replication verification. Separate sessions or native subagents are allowed;
simultaneous work and multiple providers/accounts are not required. Each task needs
inputs/versions, source locators, permitted tools/write paths, outputs, checks,
dependencies, and stopping rules. Handoffs report actual changes, commands, findings,
limitations, and unresolved decisions. Demonstrate one successful fresh-session
continuation using files alone. Verify skill discovery, explicit use, and output checks.

STEP 6 - Prepare data and reproduce basic quantities
Preserve originals. Implement exclusions, joins, coding, scoring, and analytical
samples in code. Check counts, labels, missingness, and nesting before descriptives.
If only prepared author data are supplied, state that starting point rather than
claiming to reconstruct unseen raw-data production.
Outputs: preprocessing code, validation records, descriptives, input versions.

STEP 7 - Run diagnostics and complete models
Inspect a small authorized run, then refit every required advanced model and
relevant supplementary analysis. Attribute author code and annotate its analytical
targets. Log versions, commands, times, seeds/settings where applicable, diagnostics,
and outputs. Document article/code/supplement conflicts and the selected faithful
target. Do not replace difficult models or present saved parameters as new fits.

STEP 8 - Check substantive correctness and independently verify
Test categories, identifiers, joins, samples, dependence, and model-specific rules.
Perform one harmless negative test on copied/synthetic inputs, show detection,
restore the copy, and rerun. Preserve original data and final outputs. Independently
verify one important descriptive quantity and one central advanced-model quantity
using a separate calculation/method. Repeating the author script or asking another
AI session is insufficient. Audit at least three consequential AI decisions with
source checks and student acceptance/correction; do not fabricate mistakes.
Outputs: checks, diagnostics, independent calculations, review, Checkpoint C.

STEP 9 - Compare results and explain discrepancies
For every inventory target, record published and reproduced values, difference,
tolerance/basis, status, and explanation. Classify each target as reproduced,
reproduced with discrepancy, or not verifiable from available materials. Keep
failures and unavailable inputs visible. Explain magnitude, uncertainty, alternatives,
and limitations. Distinguish numerical agreement, support for a prediction,
mechanism evidence, and broader theoretical support.

STEP 10 - Conduct a separate sensitivity analysis and design a follow-up
After faithful reproduction, change one justified specification assumption and
state whether the estimand/question changes. Report stable and changed findings
equally honestly. Write a one-page follow-up: unresolved question, theory-derived
hypothesis, design, measures, sample-size/precision rationale, analysis, interpretation.
New data collection is not required.

STEP 11 - Clean rerun, report, fixed version, and submission
Test the complete entry command from a clean state. Ensure README navigation works.
Write the English 1,000-1,200-word APA 7 report. The access block gives student name
and ID, article, full repository URL, invitation status, assessed release/tag, and
full commit. Create the fixed tag/named Release and attach the identical Moodle
report. The student personally checks complex-model evidence, access, and final
acceptance. Record Checkpoint D. Preserve the tag; later corrections are separate.

STEP 12 - Prepare for the individual oral defence
Explain a finding from theory through hypothesis, measurement, model, code, output,
and conclusion. Explain how analysis changes when measurement, dependence, or design
conditions change. The student must understand and answer personally.

7. OPTION 3 AND FINAL FORMAT NOTES

Use original independent-coder ratings for reliability, never adjudicated consensus
codes. Distinguish news items, actors/statements, and directed actor references.
Reproduce the ordinal model and its assumption checks, three-level binary models,
probability summaries, and marginal effects. Preserve published hierarchy and coding.
State the estimate scale and random-effect handling in predictions. Optional AI
recoding requires lawful original-text access and cannot replace original reliability.

MCP, Superpowers, CI, simultaneous agents, a website, an application prototype, and
new participants are not required. Select only tools that support the scientific task.

APA 7 report format: 12-point Times New Roman, black text, one-inch margins,
double-spaced prose, 0.5-inch first-line indents, page numbers, author-date citations,
and hanging reference indents. Number/title figures and tables; use minimal table
horizontal rules and no vertical gridlines. Display full DOI and web addresses.
Confirm final file format and other platform details on Moodle.

Assessment emphasizes scientific understanding, measurement/design/model alignment,
controlled agentic work, complete refitting, independent verification, original model
diagrams, transparent limitations, and personal interpretation/transfer of the logic.
